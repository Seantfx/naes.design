Bio

I'm a 33-year-old Franco-Irish designer based in Andalusia, Spain. Designing remotely for Zeliq and Nodale, bringing 10+ years of product thinking, design systems, and user-centered craft to every project.

I started designing at 14, teaching myself Photoshop to customize Counter-Strike visuals. What began as a hobby quickly turned into a business. At 16, I co-founded Shark-Config, a digital agency serving 100+ clients across e-commerce, restaurants, e-sport teams, and entrepreneurs.

After graduating from Epitech Digital in 2016, I joined 360Learning as the second designer and helped grow the design team to 8 members over nearly five years.

In 2021, I became the founding designer and first employee at Claap.io, building the entire product and brand from scratch. Claap earned Top 10 best-designed products of 2022 on ProductHunt, Product of the Day in 2022 and 2023, and was featured among the best screen recording apps in 2024. The company was later acquired by Lemlist.

In June 2026, TV Time — the app millions of people used to track their shows — announced it was shutting down within a month. My feed filled with the same question: "where do we go now?" I decided to answer it by building the replacement myself.

Version 1.0 shipped on July 3rd, twelve days before the shutdown, with the one feature that mattered on day one: drag-and-drop import of your entire TV Time history — every episode, every watch date, every rating — parsed in the browser and never uploaded. In the first month, 759 libraries and 6.7 million episodes were migrated.

From there, TV Track grew in public: a release calendar with morning notifications, watch-time stats, community reactions and threaded comments with auto-translation, 86 badges across 11 families, two-way Trakt sync, public profiles — 105 releases in 50 days, most of them shaped directly by user feedback. It is free, has no lock-in (export everything in one click) and now lives as a PWA, a native Android app and an iOS app built with Expo.

Cap and Screen Studio made polished product demos effortless — on a Mac. Framewave brings that experience to Windows, and keeps every byte on your machine: recording, editing, AI captions and export all run locally, with no account and no cloud.

Record a display, a window or a drawn region with camera, microphone and system audio. The real cursor is hidden during capture and re-drawn in the editor, so it stays crisp at any zoom. Clicks and typing automatically generate zoom keyframes with spring easing; backgrounds, padding, shadows, keystroke overlays and text animations finish the frame.

Captions are transcribed by Whisper running in-process on WebGPU. A montage mode assembles clips with transitions and an independent audio track. Export goes up to 4K in MP4, WebM or GIF, GPU-accelerated through WebCodecs — typically many times faster than real time. Every project is a plain folder: media files, an events log and a JSON edit, readable by anything.

View on GitHub

Record

One shortcut, everything captured.

Ctrl+Shift+R records a display, a window or a drawn region, with camera, microphone and system audio. The floating controls are excluded from the capture, and every click, scroll and keystroke is logged so the editor can use it later.

Auto-zoom

Zooms written by your clicks.

Every click and burst of typing becomes a zoom keyframe with a focus point, spring easing and a hold time. Adjust the feel — sensitivity, level, transition — and regenerate the whole track in one click, or place zooms by hand at the playhead.

Cursor

The cursor is redrawn, not recorded.

The real cursor is hidden during capture and re-rendered on top of the video, so it stays crisp at any zoom. Pick a style, scale it, smooth its path, let it switch to an I-beam over text — then add ripples, a spotlight or a motion trail.

Backgrounds & layout

Studio framing in two sliders.

Gradients with editable stops, wallpapers, solid colours, an image or a blurred copy of the screen itself. Padding, corner radius and shadow set the frame; seven aspect presets from 16:9 to 9:16 make it ready for any platform.

Keystrokes

Shortcuts on screen, passwords never.

Key presses recorded during capture appear as pills or keycaps on the video. A shortcuts-only mode shows Ctrl+Shift+P but hides plain typing, so a demo never leaks what you actually wrote.

AI captions

Transcribed on your GPU, not in the cloud.

Whisper runs in-process on WebGPU: three model sizes, automatic language detection, nothing sent anywhere. Captions are editable line by line, styled with a highlight colour, and exportable as SRT or VTT.

Text & badges

Titles, lower thirds and badges that move.

Four text presets, four entrance animations, full typographic control and a background pill with its own radius and shadow. Drag the text straight on the preview to place it; the timeline holds its timing.

Montage

Several clips, one story.

Drop videos and images, reorder them on the timeline, trim in and out points, change speed, detach audio to its own track. Eight transition types — cross fade, dip, slide, wipe, zoom, blur — can be applied per clip or to all at once.

Export

4K in minutes, on the GPU.

MP4, WebM with transparency, GIF, PNG sequence or MP3; 480p to 2160p, 24 to 60 fps, H.264, H.265 or AV1. Encoding goes through WebCodecs with hardware acceleration — typically many times faster than real time.

Real-time space strategy, straight from the browser.

Role

Orbital Foundry pits two to four friends against each other around a contested asteroid field. Each player owns an orbital station: mine metal and crystal, build and upgrade eight module slots, commit to an irreversible doctrine, assemble fleets — and protect your reactor, because losing it means elimination. A match lasts about 25 minutes.

The design bet is strategy over dexterity. Fleets are persistent, firing is automatic, there is no selection rectangle — every action works as a single tap, so a player on an iPad is never at a disadvantage against one on a mouse. Hidden information is filtered before it leaves the host, so a sound or a muzzle flash can never betray a ship you are not supposed to see.

Matches run peer-to-peer over WebRTC: no server, the game lives in the host's tab. Every ship silhouette, station module, asteroid and effect is generated by code at startup — the whole game downloads in 336 KB, with a 20 Hz all-integer simulation covered by 235 automated tests. It started as a 27-section product requirements document; the code is its implementation.
